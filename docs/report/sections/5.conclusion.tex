\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have proposed a hierarchical nonparametric Bayesian extension of the IDR framework that accommodates an arbitrary number of replicates, an unknown number of dependence regimes, and heterogeneous non-Gaussian marginals within a single coherent model. The reproducible-component copula is modeled as a Pitman--Yor mixture of Gaussian copulas with structured correlation, augmented with $t_5$, Clayton, and Gumbel atoms; per-replicate marginals follow Bernstein--Dirichlet priors. We provided two complementary inference algorithms (slice-sampled P\'olya-urn MCMC and structured mean-field variational inference), and established identifiability via the Yakowitz--Spragins linear-independence criterion under a positive-lower-orthant-dependence constraint, Hellinger posterior contraction at the rate $\epsilon_n = n^{-\beta/(2\beta+K)}(\log n)^t$, asymptotic Bayes-mFDR control of a Sun--Cai-type plug-in step-up rule via the rate-to-mFDR bridge of \citet{HellerRosset2021} and \citet{CastilloRoquain2020}, and Harris ergodicity of the slice sampler with a geometric-rate strengthening stated as a conjecture.

\subsection{Limitations}

Three limitations should be made explicit. First, the geometric-ergodicity conjecture in Theorem~\ref{thm:erg} is not proved in full; we establish Harris ergodicity unconditionally and conditional geometric ergodicity given that the small set $C$ contains the bulk of posterior mass on cluster configurations, but a full Foster--Lyapunov drift analysis on the infinite-dimensional configuration space remains open. The recent quantitative-mixing analysis of \citet{Ascolani2024Mixing} for related Dirichlet-process samplers offers a plausible path forward, but is not directly transferable to our setting. Second, the variational consistency result of Theorem~\ref{thm:vi} requires a finite truncation $T$ of the PY stick to invoke the parametric Bernstein--von Mises theorem of \citet{WangBlei2019}; nonparametric extension via \citet{AlquierRidgway2020} requires verification of additional regularity conditions that we have not fully discharged. Third, the LKJ($\eta = 2$) prior on within-cluster correlation matrices imposes mild concentration toward the identity, which is a deliberate weakly-informative choice but may affect inference in extremely sparse regimes.

\subsection{Practical recommendations}

For practitioners using PY-IDR on consortium-scale datasets, we offer the following recommendations. The MCMC scheme is preferred for $n \lesssim 10^4$ where exact posterior summaries with credible bands are required; the variational alternative is preferred for $n \gtrsim 10^5$ where the per-sweep cost of MCMC becomes prohibitive. The auxiliary-atom count $H = 5$ in Algorithm~\ref{alg:mcmc} is a reasonable default; reducing $H$ to 3 yields a roughly 30\% wall-clock saving with mild loss of mixing efficiency. For genomic ChIP-seq and ATAC-seq applications, the heavy-tail atomic types ($t_5$, Clayton, Gumbel) materially affect inference in the upper tail of the score distribution and should not be disabled. For ancestry-stratified GWAS applications, the hierarchical PY structure is essential and the sample-size imbalance across ancestries should be accounted for through the per-replicate Bernstein--Dirichlet marginals.

\subsection{Future work}

Several extensions are natural. The most direct extension is to vine-copula atoms in place of structured-correlation Gaussian atoms, which would allow asymmetric pairwise dependence patterns at the cost of a more demanding base measure. A second extension is to time-varying or covariate-dependent dependence regimes, in the framework of \citet{DallaValleLeisenRossiniZhu2024}, which would accommodate longitudinal reproducibility studies. A third extension is to non-exchangeable replicate structures via dependent Pitman--Yor priors, which would allow modeling of donor-stratified single-cell data with within-donor and between-donor dependence regimes. A fourth extension is to multi-resolution score representations beyond ranks, exploiting the original signal magnitudes through $z$-score-aware likelihoods. Finally, a more substantial theoretical contribution would be a complete drift-and-minorization proof of geometric ergodicity for slice-sampled PY mixtures, closing the gap identified in Theorem~\ref{thm:erg}.
