\subsection{Generative model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsubsection{Latent class and copula representation}

For $i = 1, \ldots, n$, let $Z_i \in \{0, 1\}$ be the latent reproducibility class with $\Pr(Z_i = 1) = \pi$, where $Z_i = 1$ denotes the reproducible component and $Z_i = 0$ the irreproducible component. The population uniformized vector is
\[
\bU_i = (F_1(X_{i,1}), \ldots, F_K(X_{i,K}))^\top \in [0,1]^K,
\]
where $F_j$ is the marginal CDF of replicate $j$. In the full model the $F_j$ are inferred by the Bernstein--Dirichlet marginals below. Empirical scaled ranks are used as initialization, diagnostics, and a practical rank-only approximation in sensitivity analyses; they are not a replacement for the marginal model in the theoretical statements. Conditional on $Z_i = z$, the uniformized vector has copula density
\begin{equation}
\bU_i \mid Z_i = z \sim c_z(\bU_i; \btheta_z),
\label{eq:cop-density}
\end{equation}
where, by the IDR convention, $c_0$ is the independence copula on $[0,1]^K$ and $c_1$ is the reproducible-component copula. We retain this convention throughout for compatibility with the IDR literature; weakly dependent alternatives for $c_0$ are a planned robustness extension rather than part of the present theory. The local idr is the posterior probability of the irreproducible class, $\idr_i = \Pr(Z_i = 0 \mid \bU_i)$, and the aggregated $\IDR(\gamma)$ is the marginal FDR of the rejection set $\mathcal{R}(\gamma) = \{i : \idr_i < \gamma\}$,
\begin{equation}
\IDR(\gamma) = \frac{\E\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^n (1-Z_i)\,\bm{1}\{\idr_i < \gamma\}\right]}{\E\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^n \bm{1}\{\idr_i < \gamma\}\right]},
\label{eq:agg-idr}
\end{equation}
with the convention that the ratio is undefined when the denominator is zero and is evaluated only over thresholds producing at least one rejection.

\subsubsection{Reproducible-component copula}

Conditional on $Z_i = 1$, the copula density is the Pitman--Yor mixture
\begin{equation}
c_1(\bU_i) = \sum_{m=1}^\infty w_m\, c_{\theta_m,t_m}(\bU_i),\qquad
(w_1,w_2,\ldots) \sim \GEM(\alpha,\sigma),\quad (\theta_m,t_m) \stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim} P_0,
\label{eq:py-mix}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha>0$ and $\sigma\in[0,1)$. In stick-breaking form, $V_m\sim\Beta(1-\sigma,\alpha+m\sigma)$ independently and $w_m=V_m\prod_{\ell<m}(1-V_\ell)$. Each atom comprises continuous parameters $\theta_m$ and an atomic-type indicator $t_m \in \{\text{Gauss}, t_5, \text{Clayton}, \text{Gumbel}\}$; $P_0(t)$ is uniform on the four families.

For Gaussian and $t_5$ atoms, $\theta_m$ includes a correlation matrix $R_m$. The exchangeable class uses $R=(1-\rho)I+\rho\one\one^\top$ with $0<\rho<1$. The one-factor and two-factor classes are parameterized through an unconstrained positive definite matrix $\Sigma=D+\Lambda\Lambda^\top$ and normalized to a correlation matrix $R=\operatorname{diag}(\Sigma)^{-1/2}\Sigma\operatorname{diag}(\Sigma)^{-1/2}$. For atoms subject to the PLOD restriction below, the implementation either constrains the factor loadings to a common sign or rejects/proposes again until all pairwise correlations are strictly positive. Clayton atoms use dependence parameter $\theta>0$, and Gumbel atoms use $\theta>1$ when strict separation from independence is required. We exclude vine constructions for analytical tractability and defer them to future work.

\subsubsection{Replicate-specific marginals}

Per-replicate marginals $F_j$ have Bernstein--Dirichlet priors,
\begin{equation}
F_j(x) = \sum_{r=1}^{M_j} p_{j,r}\, B_r\!\left(F_j^{(0)}(x); M_j\right),\qquad
B_r(t;M)=I_t(r,M-r+1),\qquad \bm p_j\sim\Dir(\alpha_F\one_{M_j}),
\label{eq:bernstein}
\end{equation}
where $B_r(t;M)$ is the cumulative Bernstein basis of order $M_j$ at index $r$, identified with the regularized incomplete beta function; $F_j^{(0)}$ is a coarse pilot CDF; and $\alpha_F>0$ is a smoothing hyperparameter. The Bernstein--Dirichlet construction is canonical for densities on $[0,1]$ \citep{Petrone1999,PetroneWasserman2002}. In implementation, ties in the raw scores are handled by mid-ranks for initialization and by the pilot-CDF value in the likelihood contribution.

\subsubsection{Identifiability constraint}

The reproducible-component mixture is constrained to atoms with positive lower-orthant dependence (PLOD): for every atom index $m$,
\begin{equation}
C_{\theta_m,t_m}(u,\ldots,u) > u^K \quad \text{for all } u\in(0,1),
\label{eq:plod}
\end{equation}
where $C_{\theta_m,t_m}$ is the copula function corresponding to density $c_{\theta_m,t_m}$. The PLOD condition \citep[Definition 2.1]{Joe1997} is satisfied by Gaussian and $t$ copulas with strictly positive pairwise correlations and by Clayton and Gumbel copulas away from their independence boundary. PLOD alone distinguishes strict positive-dependence atoms from the independence copula but does not by itself identify the mixing weight $\pi$ in an arbitrary infinite mixture; the identifiability theorem below therefore adds an explicit separation and finite-sieve linear-independence condition.

\subsubsection{Hyperpriors}
\label{sec:hyperpriors}

We place $\pi \sim \Beta(1,1)$, $\alpha\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(1,1)$, and $\sigma\sim\mathrm{Uniform}[0,1)$, the last specification being equivalent to a flat prior on the admissible discount interval. The Bernstein smoothing hyperparameter satisfies $\alpha_F\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(1,1)$. The Bernstein degree $M_j$ has a discrete prior on $\{5,10,20,40,80\}$ with weights proportional to $1/M_j$,
\begin{equation}
\Pr(M_j=m)=\frac{1/m}{\sum_{m'\in\{5,10,20,40,80\}}1/m'},\qquad m\in\{5,10,20,40,80\}.
\label{eq:m-prior}
\end{equation}
For unstructured Gaussian-copula atoms we use an LKJ($\eta=2$) prior \citep{LewandowskiKurowickaJoe2009} on the correlation matrix. Structured Gaussian atoms draw a structural-class indicator uniformly over the three classes (exchangeable, one-factor, two-factor), while the atomic-type indicator is uniform over the four copula families. Thus the structural support has size three and the atomic-type support has size four.

\subsection{Inference}
\label{sec:inference}

\subsubsection{P\'olya-urn auxiliary-atom MCMC}

We use a Pitman--Yor P\'olya-urn MCMC scheme with Neal-style auxiliary atoms rather than a stick-breaking slice sampler. This choice avoids maintaining an explicit infinite stick during cluster reassignment while retaining the Pitman--Yor predictive distribution \citep{Pitman1996BlackwellMacQueen,IshwaranJames2001}. Algorithm~\ref{alg:mcmc} details one full sweep. The class-assignment probability is
\begin{equation}
\widehat\pi_i = \Pr(Z_i=1\mid \bU_i,\btheta,\bw,\bm F)=
\frac{\pi\,c_1(\bU_i;\btheta,\bw)}{\pi\,c_1(\bU_i;\btheta,\bw)+(1-\pi)c_0(\bU_i)},
\label{eq:cond-class}
\end{equation}
where $c_0(\bU_i)=1$. In a collapsed implementation, $c_1$ is evaluated through the current occupied clusters and the PY predictive rather than by truncating the infinite stick.

For cluster reassignment, let $n_{m,-i}$ be the number of reproducible observations assigned to occupied cluster $m$ after temporarily removing observation $i$, and let $T_{-i}$ be the number of occupied clusters. Draw $H$ auxiliary atoms $\widetilde\theta_1,\ldots,\widetilde\theta_H\sim P_0$. Then the conditional weights are
\begin{align}
\Pr(z_i=m\mid\cdot) &\propto (n_{m,-i}-\sigma)\,c_{\theta_m,t_m}(\bU_i),\qquad m=1,\ldots,T_{-i},\label{eq:py-occupied}\\
\Pr(z_i=\widetilde m_h\mid\cdot) &\propto \frac{\alpha+\sigma T_{-i}}{H}\,c_{\widetilde\theta_h,\widetilde t_h}(\bU_i),\qquad h=1,\ldots,H.\label{eq:py-new}
\end{align}
The occupied and auxiliary weights are normalized jointly; choosing an auxiliary atom instantiates a new occupied cluster.

The Bernstein-weight update is conjugate after introducing latent basis-membership indicators $S_{i,j}\in\{1,\ldots,M_j\}$ with
\begin{equation}
\Pr(S_{i,j}=r\mid\cdot)\propto p_{j,r}\,b_r(F_j^{(0)}(X_{i,j});M_j),
\end{equation}
where $b_r(t;M)=M\binom{M-1}{r-1}t^{r-1}(1-t)^{M-r}$ is the Bernstein density basis. The conditional posterior is $\bm p_j\mid\cdot\sim\Dir(\alpha_F+n_{j,1},\ldots,\alpha_F+n_{j,M_j})$ with $n_{j,r}=\#\{i:S_{i,j}=r\}$.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\DontPrintSemicolon
\caption{One sweep of Pitman--Yor P\'olya-urn MCMC for PY-IDR.}
\label{alg:mcmc}
\KwIn{Current state $(\pi,Z,z,\theta,t,\bm F,\alpha,\sigma)$, $H$ auxiliary atoms, observed scores $\bX_{1:n}$.}
\KwOut{Updated state.}
\textbf{Class assignment.} For each $i$, draw $Z_i\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\widehat\pi_i)$ using Eq.~\eqref{eq:cond-class}\;
\textbf{Cluster reassignment.} For each $i$ with $Z_i=1$, temporarily remove $i$ from its cluster, draw $H$ auxiliary atoms from $P_0$, and sample $z_i$ using Eqs.~\eqref{eq:py-occupied}--\eqref{eq:py-new}\;
\textbf{Atom-parameter update.} For each occupied cluster, update continuous parameters using NUTS or Metropolis proposals on a valid unconstrained parameterization, normalizing covariance factors to correlation matrices and rejecting proposals outside the PLOD support\;
\textbf{Atomic-type update.} For each occupied cluster, update $t_m$ by independence Metropolis with a uniform proposal over the four atomic families\;
\textbf{Marginal update.} Sample latent basis-membership indicators $S_{i,j}$ and update $\bm p_j\sim\Dir(\alpha_F+n_{j,1},\ldots,\alpha_F+n_{j,M_j})$\;
\textbf{Marginal-degree update.} Update $(M_j,\alpha_F)$ by Metropolis-within-Gibbs with random-walk proposals on $\log\alpha_F$ and a discrete proposal on $M_j\in\{5,10,20,40,80\}$\;
\textbf{PY hyperparameter update.} Update $\alpha$ and $\sigma$ with auxiliary-variable or slice updates on their admissible supports\;
\textbf{Mixing-proportion update.} Sample $\pi\sim\Beta(1+n_1,1+n_0)$ where $n_z=\#\{i:Z_i=z\}$\;
\Return{Updated state.}\;
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Initialisation and diagnostics.}
Each chain is initialised from a pairwise Vanilla-IDR EM solution aggregated
across the $\binom{K}{2}$ replicate pairs: the marginal $\pi$ and $\rho$ are
recovered as the mean of the per-pair EM estimates, and per-feature class
indicators $Z$ are recovered by thresholding the aggregated per-pair local
idr at $1/2$. The implementation diagnostics layer follows §3.3.1 of the
empirical protocol: a deterministic seed ledger, rank-tie summary,
overdispersed chain starts, split-$\widehat R\leq 1.01$ thresholding, an
effective-sample-size threshold of $400$ for reported scalar summaries,
cluster-count trace plots, posterior label-invariant summaries
(descending-sorted cluster-size vector with the Wasserstein-1 distance
to a user-supplied reference; an iterative relabelling step in the
spirit of \citet{Stephens2000Relabel} is available for per-cluster scalar
summaries), and a divergent-transition fraction aggregated from the
per-sweep NUTS atom and $(\alpha,\sigma)$ updates. Sensitivity to the auxiliary-atom count $H$ is a
planned ablation; the default in the package is $H{=}5$.

\paragraph{Complexity.}
Each MCMC sweep is $O(nKT+K^3T)$ when all active clusters are evaluated for each feature, where $T$ is the number of active clusters. The first term is dominated by per-cluster copula-density evaluation and the second by correlation-matrix operations. If a balanced tree or cached nearest-cluster screen is used in a future implementation, an additional logarithmic factor may appear; the analytic baseline used here does not require it.

\subsubsection{Variational alternative}
\label{sec:vi}

A structured mean-field family with finite truncation $T$ at the PY stick yields an ELBO optimized by stochastic natural-gradient ascent in the framework of \citet{HoffmanBleiWangPaisley2013}. The planned variational family is
\begin{align*}
q(Z,z,\theta,t,V,\pi,F,\alpha,\sigma)
&=\prod_i q(Z_i)\prod_i q(z_i)\prod_{m\leq T}q(\theta_m)\prod_{m\leq T}q(t_m)\\
&\quad\times\prod_{m\leq T}q(V_m)q(\pi)\prod_j q(F_j)q(\alpha)q(\sigma),
\end{align*}
where $q(Z_i)$ and $q(z_i)$ are categorical, $q(\theta_m)$ is Gaussian or transformed-Gaussian on the unconstrained atomic parameters, $q(t_m)$ is categorical over the four atomic types, $q(V_m)$ is Beta on the stick-breaking ratios, $q(\pi)$ is Beta, and $q(F_j)$ is Dirichlet on Bernstein weights. Per-iteration cost is $O(BKT\bar d)$ for mini-batch size $B$ and mean atomic-parameter dimension $\bar d$. We treat the variational algorithm as an implementation-oriented approximation: MCMC is the default for $n\lesssim10^4$ when posterior uncertainty is central, and VI is planned for $n\gtrsim10^5$ when approximate summaries are preferable to prohibitive MCMC cost.

\subsubsection{Local idr, aggregated IDR, and Bayes-mFDR}

The posterior local idr is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\idr}_i=\Pr(Z_i=0\mid\mathrm{data})=
\int \Pr(Z_i=0\mid\mathrm{data},\btheta,\bw,\bm F)\,\Pi(d\btheta,d\bw,d\bm F\mid\mathrm{data}).
\label{eq:idr}
\end{equation}
The Bayes marginal false discovery rate at threshold $\gamma$ is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Bayes\text{-}mFDR}(\gamma)=
\frac{\E[V(\gamma)\mid\mathrm{data}]}{\max\{1,\E[R(\gamma)\mid\mathrm{data}]\}},
\label{eq:bayes-mfdr}
\end{equation}
where $V(\gamma)=\sum_i(1-Z_i)\bm{1}\{\widehat\idr_i<\gamma\}$ and $R(\gamma)=\sum_i\bm{1}\{\widehat\idr_i<\gamma\}$. Operationally, sort $\widehat\idr_{(1)}\leq\cdots\leq\widehat\idr_{(n)}$ and define
\begin{equation}
\widehat k_\alpha=\max\left\{k\geq1:\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^k \widehat\idr_{(j)}\leq \alpha\right\},
\label{eq:step-up}
\end{equation}
with no rejections if the set is empty. The rejection set consists of the $\widehat k_\alpha$ features with smallest posterior local idr. This is the empirical-Bayes plug-in counterpart of the optimal compound-decision rule of \citet{SunCai2007}.

\subsection{Theoretical properties}
\label{sec:theory}

We state results on identifiability, posterior contraction, asymptotic mFDR control, sampler ergodicity, and finite-truncation variational consistency. The statements are deliberately conditional where the current manuscript uses assumptions or proof sketches rather than complete proofs.

\subsubsection{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[Smooth bounded truth]
\label{ass:smooth}
The true reproducible-component copula density $c_1^*$ is $\beta$-H\"older with $\beta\geq1$ on $[0,1]^K$ and is bounded above by $B<\infty$. The true marginal densities $f_j^*$ are $\beta$-H\"older on their supports and are bounded away from zero on compact interior subsets.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Mass and separation]
\label{ass:pi}
The true reproducible mass satisfies $\pi^*\in(0,1)$ and is bounded away from $\{0,1\}$. The independence component is separated from the reproducible component in the sense that the reproducible mixing measure assigns no mass to independence-boundary atoms and satisfies the finite-sieve linear-independence condition in Theorem~\ref{thm:id}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Prior KL support]
\label{ass:kl}
For every $\epsilon>0$, the prior on the reproducible-component copula assigns positive mass to KL neighborhoods of $c_1^*$, and each Bernstein--Dirichlet marginal prior assigns positive mass to KL neighborhoods of $f_j^*$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Sieve and tail condition]
\label{ass:py}
There exist sieves $\mathcal F_n$ satisfying
\[
\log N(\epsilon_n,\mathcal F_n,h)\lesssim n\epsilon_n^2,
\qquad
\Pi(\mathcal F_n^c)\lesssim \exp\{-(C+4)n\epsilon_n^2\}.
\]
For the Dirichlet-process special case $\sigma=0$, exponential stick-tail bounds provide one route to this condition. For fixed positive Pitman--Yor discount, the required exponential sieve-complement bound is treated here as an explicit assumption or future proof obligation rather than a consequence of the polynomial residual expectation alone.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Decision-threshold regularity]
\label{ass:decision}
The oracle local-idr distribution has a continuous density in a neighborhood of the target Sun--Cai threshold, and the expected number of rejections at that threshold is asymptotically bounded away from zero at the scale used in the mFDR ratio.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Sampler regularity]
\label{ass:slice}
The P\'olya-urn MCMC transition kernel is irreducible, aperiodic, and leaves the posterior invariant on each finite truncation/sieve used for computation. For geometric-rate conclusions, a separate small-set and Foster--Lyapunov drift condition is required.
\end{assumption}

\subsubsection{Identifiability}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-sieve identifiability]
\label{thm:id}
Consider a finite sieve in which the reproducible copula is a finite mixture of atoms satisfying PLOD, no atom lies on the independence boundary, and the collection consisting of the independence copula density together with the reproducible atom densities is linearly independent. Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:smooth} and~\ref{ass:pi}, the joint $(\pi,c_0,c_1,F_1,\ldots,F_K)$ is identifiable up to permutation of the reproducible atom labels.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Sklar's theorem \citep{Sklar1959} identifies the marginals $F_j$ from the joint distribution of $\bX$ under the stated regularity conditions, reducing the problem to the copula mixture $(1-\pi)c_0+\pi c_1$. PLOD separates admissible reproducible atoms from the independence boundary, but the mixing weight is recovered by the finite-mixture identifiability argument rather than by a diagonal-section derivative. Under the stated linear independence of $\{c_0,c_{\theta_1,t_1},\ldots,c_{\theta_T,t_T}\}$, the criterion of \citet{YakowitzSpragins1968} implies uniqueness of the finite mixing representation up to label permutation. Gaussian copula densities with distinct admissible correlation matrices are analytic on the open cube, and the heavy-tail families are treated as separate linearly independent families on the finite sieve. Extension to unrestricted infinite PY mixtures requires additional completeness or weak-identifiability assumptions on the mixing measure.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:amr-vs-yakowitz}
We use \citet{YakowitzSpragins1968} rather than \citet{AllmanMatiasRhodes2009} as the primary finite-sieve identifiability tool. The latter's Kruskal-based theorem requires conditional independence of the observed variables given the latent class, which is violated by correlated copula-mixture components.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Posterior contraction}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional Hellinger posterior contraction]
\label{thm:contract}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:smooth}--\ref{ass:py}, the posterior contracts in Hellinger distance at rate $\epsilon_n=n^{-\beta/(2\beta+K)}(\log n)^t$ for some $t>0$:
\[
\Pi\!\left(h(f,f^*)>M\epsilon_n\mid\bX_{1:n}\right)\to0\qquad\text{in }P^*\text{-probability,}
\]
for sufficiently large $M$, where $f$ is the joint density implied by the model and $f^*$ is the truth.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
The result is an application of the master contraction theorem of \citet{GhosalvdV2017} once prior mass, entropy, and sieve-complement conditions are verified. The KL-support condition is supplied by Assumption~\ref{ass:kl} together with Gaussian-copula approximation on the bounded smooth copula class and Bernstein--Dirichlet support for the marginals. Lemma~\ref{lem:sklar-regularity} and Lemma~\ref{lem:mixent} give the required copula/marginal comparison under bounded-density assumptions. Lemma~\ref{lem:cover} supplies the finite-sieve entropy calculation. The exponential sieve-complement probability is not implied by the positive-discount PY residual expectation alone and is therefore exactly the tail condition isolated in Assumption~\ref{ass:py}. Combining these ingredients yields the displayed $K$-dimensional nonparametric rate.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the contraction theorem]
\label{rem:bounded-scope}
The theorem covers bounded smooth copula truths. Tail-dependent atoms such as Clayton, Gumbel, and $t_5$ copulas are part of the proposed methodology and planned robustness evaluation, but boundary-singular copula densities require additional theory beyond the bounded interior statement above.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Gaussian-only base measure]
\label{cor:gauss-only}
If the base measure $P_0$ is restricted to bounded Gaussian-copula atoms and Assumption~\ref{ass:py} is verified for the resulting sieve, the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:contract} holds with the same rate.
\end{corollary}

\subsubsection{Asymptotic Bayes-mFDR control}

\begin{theorem}[Asymptotic Bayes-mFDR control]
\label{thm:fdr}
Suppose Theorem~\ref{thm:contract} holds and that Assumption~\ref{ass:decision} holds. If the posterior local idr estimates satisfy a uniform or threshold-local error bound $\delta_n\to0$, then the Sun--Cai-type step-up rule in Eq.~\eqref{eq:step-up} controls Bayes-mFDR at level $\alpha+O(\delta_n)$. When the available posterior contraction result gives only $L^1$ local-idr consistency, the conclusion is convergence of the plug-in Bayes-mFDR to the oracle mFDR rather than a sharp uniform rate.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
The oracle Sun--Cai rule controls the target mFDR under the compound-decision model \citep{SunCai2007}. A uniform or threshold-local local-idr error bound transfers the oracle rejection threshold to the plug-in threshold by continuity of the local-idr distribution at the target cutoff. The denominator lower-bound condition in Assumption~\ref{ass:decision} prevents instability when the rejection set is empty or nearly empty. The rate-to-mFDR bridge is consistent with the empirical-Bayes multiple-testing analyses of \citet{HellerRosset2021} and \citet{CastilloRoquain2020}, but the precise rate is only as strong as the local-idr error bound established for the present model.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Ergodicity of the sampler}

\begin{theorem}[Harris ergodicity and conditional geometric ergodicity]
\label{thm:erg}
Under the invariance, irreducibility, and aperiodicity conditions in Assumption~\ref{ass:slice}, the finite-sieve P\'olya-urn MCMC kernel is Harris ergodic with stationary distribution equal to the posterior restricted to the computational sieve. If, in addition, a small-set condition and a Foster--Lyapunov drift condition hold for the chosen finite-dimensional parameterization, then the kernel is $V$-uniformly geometrically ergodic.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
Harris ergodicity follows from standard general-state-space Markov chain theory \citep{MeynTweedie2009,RobertsRosenthal2004}: the auxiliary-atom step gives positive probability of moving between neighboring cluster configurations, continuous atom proposals have positive density on admissible parameter neighborhoods, and the Gibbs/Metropolis composition leaves the posterior invariant. Geometric ergodicity is stronger; it requires a small-set and drift analysis on the selected finite representation. We therefore state it conditionally rather than as a completed result for the unrestricted PY mixture. Recent quantitative-mixing results for nonparametric mixture samplers \citep{Ascolani2024Mixing} indicate a plausible route but do not directly prove the present claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-truncation variational consistency]
\label{thm:vi}
Fix a truncation level $T<\infty$ and suppose the truncated model is correctly specified or approximates the truth with vanishing truncation error as $T=T_n$ grows. If the exact posterior for the truncated model contracts and the structured variational family contains distributions within $o(n\epsilon_n^2)$ KL divergence of that posterior, then the variational posterior predictive density is Hellinger consistent. Matching the rate in Theorem~\ref{thm:contract} requires the additional variational approximation and truncation assumptions used in \citet{AlquierRidgway2020}, \citet{YangPatiBhattacharya2020}, and \citet{WangBlei2019}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
For fixed finite truncation, the problem is finite-dimensional after the correlation and marginal-degree parameterizations are fixed, and variational consistency follows from standard posterior concentration plus the variational KL approximation condition. For a growing truncation, the residual stick mass and the approximation error of the structured mean-field projection must both be controlled. These are recorded as assumptions rather than asserted as completed proof steps for the unrestricted PY-copula model.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Theory-to-experiment bridge.}
The planned experiments are designed to stress-test the assumptions above. S1--S3 evaluate calibration and contraction in Gaussian settings close to the bounded theory; S4 tests marginal misspecification and the Bernstein--Dirichlet correction; S5 tests the methodological value of tail-dependent atoms beyond the bounded-theory scope; PY-versus-DP ablations test rare-cluster behavior; and Bayes-mFDR calibration plots evaluate the threshold-transfer assumptions in Theorem~\ref{thm:fdr}.
