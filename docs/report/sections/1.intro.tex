\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The IDR framework and its limitations}

The Irreproducible Discovery Rate framework of \citet{LiBrownHuangBickel2011} has become a standard tool for replicate-experiment reproducibility quantification. It posits a two-component mixture in which the reproducible component is generated by a bivariate latent Gaussian model with shared mean and variance across replicates, fits the parameters by an EM procedure on pseudo-data obtained from a marginal probability-integral transform, and reports a per-feature local idr together with a Sun--Cai step-up cutoff that controls the marginal false discovery rate. The framework is rank-invariant, scale-invariant, and threshold-free, which makes it a natural choice for consortium pipelines that aggregate heterogeneous platforms.

The original framework, however, has three rigid assumptions that modern multi-replicate designs routinely violate. First, the construction is bivariate: exactly two replicates ($K = 2$) are accommodated, while modern ENCODE 4 transcription-factor experiments typically use $K \in \{2, 3, 4, 6\}$ replicates, and large-scale algorithmic and tissue-replicate studies report on the order of tens of replicate-like inputs.\footnote{Replicate counts are approximate and vary by experiment within each consortium; we use indicative ranges drawn from consortium release notes.} When this assumption is violated by aggregating bivariate IDR pairwise across replicate pairs, downstream FDR is systematically inflated. Second, the reproducible component imposes an exchangeable bivariate Gaussian dependence with a single correlation parameter; consortium data with heterogeneous platforms exhibit factor-like correlation patterns that are not exchangeable, and forcing exchangeability biases the local idr toward zero on platform-discordant features. Third, the bivariate Gaussian latent structure imposes a single shared marginal Gaussian latent variance across replicates after pseudo-data transformation; replicates from different sequencing depths or tissue types have markedly different marginal distributions, and the shared-variance assumption produces miscalibrated tail-region idr estimates exactly where reproducibility decisions are made.

\begin{takeawaybox}
The original IDR was designed for two replicates with shared latent dependence and shared marginals. Modern reproducibility data routinely violate all three assumptions. The proposed PY-IDR framework relaxes each by replacing the parametric copula with a Pitman--Yor mixture, the shared correlation with structured per-cluster correlation matrices, and the shared marginals with replicate-specific Bernstein--Dirichlet priors.
\end{takeawaybox}

\subsection{Why Pitman--Yor}

A natural Bayesian extension uses a nonparametric mixture over copula structures. The Pitman--Yor process \citep{PitmanYor1997} generalizes the Dirichlet process by introducing a discount parameter $\sigma \in [0,1)$, yielding power-law cluster-size distributions that match heavy-tailed empirical regimes. The operative property motivating PY for our application is the growth rate of the expected number of distinct components: under a Dirichlet process with concentration $\alpha$ and $n$ observations, the expected number of clusters grows logarithmically as $\alpha \log n$, while under a Pitman--Yor process with discount $\sigma > 0$ it grows polynomially as $\alpha\, n^\sigma$ \citep{PitmanYor1997}. As an illustration, at $n = 10^4$ with $\alpha = 1$, a DP places its prior mass on roughly nine clusters in expectation, while a PY with $\sigma = 0.5$ places its prior mass on roughly one hundred clusters; this polynomial-versus-logarithmic gap is qualitatively important for consortium-scale reproducibility data, where dependence regimes proliferate with sample size rather than saturate.

We extend the PY mixture framework from densities on Euclidean space to mixtures of Gaussian copulas with structured correlation, augmented with tail-dependence atoms. PY mixtures of densities are well-studied with $L^1$-consistency results \citep{IshwaranJames2001,WuGhosal2010} and adaptive contraction theory in $L^p$-metrics \citep{Scricciolo2014BAPY}; the corresponding consistency theory for PY mixtures of \emph{copulas} is, to our knowledge, new. The closest published competitor is the very recent work of \citet{PanNietoBarajasCraiu2024}, who develop a Bayesian nonparametric mixture of Archimedean copulas with a Pitman--Yor mixing distribution. Our contribution differs from theirs in three central respects: we handle Gaussian copulas with structured correlation matrices rather than Archimedean families with scalar dependence parameters, we focus on the multi-replicate IDR application rather than dependence modeling per se, and we provide explicit posterior contraction rates with the dimension $K$ appearing in the standard nonparametric exponent.

\subsection{Contributions}

This paper makes the following contributions, which we summarize briefly here and develop in subsequent sections.

We propose a Pitman--Yor copula-mixture model for the reproducible component of an IDR mixture, accommodating arbitrary $K \geq 2$, structured correlation across mixture atoms (exchangeable, one-factor, two-factor), and heavy-tail atomic types ($t_5$, Clayton, Gumbel) within a single nonparametric prior. We add replicate-specific Bernstein--Dirichlet marginal priors that decouple the marginal models across replicates while retaining a tractable adaptive contraction rate. We provide two complementary inference algorithms: a slice-sampled P\'olya-urn MCMC scheme using Neal's Algorithm~8 \citep{Neal2000} extended to the Pitman--Yor predictive of \citet{Pitman1996BlackwellMacQueen} and \citet{IshwaranJames2001}, with NUTS on the Cholesky parameterization for moderate-scale problems, and a structured mean-field variational algorithm with stochastic natural gradients in the framework of \citet{HoffmanBleiWangPaisley2013} for genomic-scale data. We give a theoretical analysis establishing identifiability up to label permutation under a positive-lower-orthant-dependence constraint, Hellinger posterior contraction at a near-minimax rate, asymptotic Bayes-mFDR control of a Sun--Cai-type plug-in step-up rule, and Harris ergodicity of the slice sampler with a geometric-rate conjecture. We design an empirical protocol that benchmarks the proposed method against vanilla IDR \citep{LiBrownHuangBickel2011}, eCV \citep{Iakovidis2024eCV}, nestedIDR \citep{Ranalli2026nestedIDR}, MaRR \citep{Philtron2018MaRR}, IDR2D \citep{Krismer2020IDR2D}, ChIP-R \citep{Newell2021ChIPR}, and the recent BNP-Archimedean copula mixture of \citet{PanNietoBarajasCraiu2024}, evaluated on five real datasets and an extended S1--S5 simulation suite.

\paragraph{Relation to prior work.}
Among prior multi-replicate IDR variants, our method is the first to combine an arbitrary number of replicates, an unknown number of dependence regimes, heterogeneous non-Gaussian marginals, and asymptotic theoretical guarantees in a single coherent Bayesian framework. The eCV extension of \citet{Iakovidis2024eCV} accommodates $K \geq 3$ via aggregated coefficients of variation but retains a parametric mixture over a single dependence regime. The nestedIDR framework of \citet{Ranalli2026nestedIDR} introduces hierarchical replicability across sources but assumes Gaussian latent dependence within each source. MaRR \citep{Philtron2018MaRR} provides a frequentist nonparametric alternative based on maximum rank reproducibility but does not yield posterior uncertainty. IDR2D \citep{Krismer2020IDR2D} extends the bivariate framework to genomic interactions, and ChIP-R \citep{Newell2021ChIPR} extends to $K \geq 3$ replicates via rank-product aggregation but without explicit dependence modeling. The recent NPMLE replicability method of \citet{Yi2024NPMLE} is a frequentist alternative without explicit dependence modeling. Compared to the BNP-copula literature (e.g., \citealp{BurdaProkhorov2014,ZhuangDiaoYi2022,FeldmanKowal2024NPCopula,PanNietoBarajasCraiu2024}), our paper is the first to apply Pitman--Yor mixtures of \emph{Gaussian} copulas to the reproducibility problem, with theoretical guarantees specific to the IDR setting.
